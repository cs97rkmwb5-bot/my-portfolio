Role

I independently completed the Chaoshan folk-custom research, gameplay planning, and the IP, scene, and UI design. Every character, prop, and environment was drawn by hand, from sketch through to finished pixel art.

Jimeng was used only for early inspiration and did not produce final artwork. Cursor helped generate part of the game code, which I then revised and debugged into a fully playable demo.

About the project

This casual runner takes its cue from the Chaoshan red rice cake, a local folk symbol with real cultural weight. It is aimed at young casual players who like pixel games and have a mild curiosity about regional folk culture.

Core play includes collecting items, dodging obstacles, and a folk-custom quiz, with a finished question bank.

The playable demo includes a hand-drawn pixel kitchen as the main scene, with a complete set of character, prop, and ingredient assets. An arcade-building level, an encyclopedia, and cultural merchandise remain ideas for later. No finished visuals exist for those yet.

Map-Path Correction

AI-generated map layouts struggled to read movement logic and caused the character to clip through geometry.

I built three-color marker layers—table, floor, and water basin—to constrain and correct the paths. Once the paths checked out, I removed the markers and drew the final tile map by hand.

Reflection

AI has a limited grasp of niche local folk culture, so every visual asset was drawn by hand to keep the cultural symbols from going wrong.

Code generated by Cursor often needed several rounds of manual fixes on the interaction logic.

A runner leaves limited room for educational content. A built-in encyclopedia could carry deeper folk-custom notes in a later version. The arcade-building scene is a planned extra level.

This is an unreleased personal prototype. There is no live-user data yet.
